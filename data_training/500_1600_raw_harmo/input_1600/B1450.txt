Michelle is 44 years old, referred by Dr. Al Bishop, 3001 Brown Trail
#101, Bedford, TX 76021. She had previous surgery for a hysterectomy,
bladder suspension, breast augmentation, implant removal and lift.


1.  Headaches.

2.  Jaw pain.

3.  Clicking and popping when talking or eating.

She has had no treatment for the joint problems except she is under
orthodontic treatment. She began orthodontic treatment in March of 2006.
She has actually had nightguards to help to try and control her
clenching and bruxism since the age of 28. She has worn them almost
continuously since then and she has been through 4 splints. She gets
earaches that are getting worse and tinnitus is getting worse. She gets
vertigo sometimes but has no hearing loss. Other joints that bother her
include her hips, fingers, neck and feet. She rates her TMJ daily pain
at a level of 7, average daily facial pain at level of 7, jaw function
and diet at 0 and disability at 3. She rates her headaches at level of 8
and she gets these in the frontal, temporal and posterior head area.


1.  Norvasc 10 mg.

2.  Triamp/HCTZ 37.5 mg.

3.  Zyrtec 10 mg.

4.  Acyclovir 200 mg.

5.  Multiple vitamins.

6.  Calcium citrate.

7.  Advil.

8.  Excedrin Migraine for her severe headaches.

In frontal view, Michelle has fairly good left to right facial symmetry.
Upper lip length is about 20 mm. Upper tooth-to-lip relationship is
about 3 mm. Maxillary dental midline is about 2 mm off to the left side.
Mandibular dental midline may be 1 mm off to the left. Chin is pretty
much on the facial midline. In profile, Michelle has fairly good facial
balance. Intraorally, she is missing mandibular right second bicuspid.
She has the rest of her teeth back through the second molars. She has a
crown on the left second molar. Significant lower arch asymmetry near
the left cuspid that is probably 5 mm anterior to the right cuspid. In
fact, the right cuspid aligns better with the first bicuspid. In the
upper arch Michelle as all the teeth back through the second molars. She
has a bridge in the upper arch placed in the second bicuspid and the
first molar. She has a slight Class III end-on cuspid relationship on
the left side being about 2 mm Class III. On the right side she has a
full Class II cuspid relationship. She has an anterior deep bite of
about 1 mm. She has about a 1 mm overjet. Oropharyngeal tissues look
healthy. Uvula, soft palate and faucial pillars all appear normal.
Intranasally, the septum is deviated off to the left side. Turbinates
appear normal to moderate in size and there appears to be adequate
airway. She does report some moderate snoring and some mild to moderate
daytime somnolence.

She is not aware of having any sleep apnea type symptoms. To palpation
she has some moderate discomfort of masseters, TMJs and mild tenderness
over the SCMs, styloid areas and temporalis muscles. On opening, she has
definite consistent pop in the right joint about 15 mm of opening and
about 10 mm of closing. On the left side it was a little bit more
inconsistent and a little harder to pick up but she seemed to get a
click at times at about 10 mm of opening and 5 mm of closing. Maximal
incisal opening is 50 mm. Opening without discomfort 30 mm. Excursive
movements were 10 mm to the right and 9 mm to the left. If Michelle lays
on her back, she cannot sleep in that position at night as she blocks
off her nasal airway. She says she does not sleep much on her back.
Michelle thinks that her snoring mostly occurs when she is breathing
through her mouth.


Panorex shows the absence of all 4 third molars and the mandibular right
second bicuspid. She is also missing the maxillary left second bicuspid
and first molar replaced by a 4-unit bridge. She has root canals on the
maxillary left first bicuspid and second molar. She also has a root
canal of mandibular left second molar.

Waters view shows that she has some deviation of the bony septum.
Turbinates appear moderate in size. Sinuses appear relatively clear.

Left TMJ tomogram shows a condyle that is a little bit flattened on the
anterior leading edge. Articular eminence is moderately steep. Joint
space looks reasonably good.

Right TMJ tomogram shows a condyle that appears to have fairly good
morphology. Joint space actually is normal and articular eminence is
moderately steep. On opening, there is good translation of both
condyles.

Lateral cephalometric radiograph shows that she has a slight skeletal
Class II occlusal relationship. She appears to have a high occlusal
plane angle. Oropharyngeal airway looks reasonably good with it being
almost 10 mm at the soft palate level and probably 8 or 9 mm at the far
base of the tongue. There seems to be good space between the superior
aspect of the tongue in the inferior aspect of the palate.


1.  Bilateral TMJ arthritis and articular disc dislocation at the

2.  TMJ pain.

3.  Headaches.

4.  Myofascial pain.

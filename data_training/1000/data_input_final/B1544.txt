CLINICAL EXAMINATION. In frontal view, Margaret has fairly good
left-to-right facial symmetry. Maxillary and mandibular midline are all
pretty much on the facial midline. Chin may be just slightly to the left
side a couple of millimeters. In profile, Margaret has fairly good
facial balance. In the mandibular right quadrant and maxillary left and
right quadrant, she is missing all bicuspids and molar teeth. On the
mandibular left side, she is missing only the third molar, but has
crowns on the first and second bicuspid and first and second molars. She
is basically a Class I cuspid molar relationship. She has relatively an
anterior deep bite. Oropharyngeal tissues look relatively normal.
Intranasally, the turbinates are moderate in size, but she states she
has no significant problems breathing through her nose. She does have
some mild snoring and moderate daytime somnolence, but is not aware of
any sleep apnea issues. Since taking the Klonopin, she sleeps fairly
well at night. She is not aware of mouth breathing. To palpation, she
has very high moderate pain over the right TMJ, right SCM, right
posterior neck, and bilateral shoulders. She has moderate pain over the
masseter, right medial pterygoid, left SCM, left posterior neck, and
moderate tenderness over the left TMJ, left masseter, left medial
pterygoid and the retromandibular areas. Maximal incisal opening is
about 45 mm, opening without significant pain to 35 mm. Excursion
movements are about 10 mm in each direction, but were painful,
particularly on excursion towards the right side.

RADIOGRAPHIC EVALUATION. Panorex shows the absence in bilateral maxilla
and right mandibular of all bicuspids and molars. On the left side, just
the third molar is missing. There are crowns on the second bicuspid and
first and second molar. Turbinates appear fairly large. Sinuses appear
clear. Both joints appear arthritic with sclerosis on the head. Both
have a hockey stick shape to them.

Coronal view shows the sinuses are clear and turbinates are fairly
large.

Axial view shows that turbinates are moderately large, but sinuses are
clear.

Left TMJ sagittal view shows a condyle that appears arthritic. There is
some sclerosis on the head of the condyle. The condyle is posteriorly
positioned in the fossa with decreased posterior and superior joint
space, but with an increased anterior joint space where the articular
eminence is moderately steep. There appears to be a little osteophyte
sticking out from the articular eminence. The head of the condyle is
somewhat sclerotic. There appears to be some peaking of the condyles in
a couple of the views, particularly towards the lateral aspect of the
joint.

Right TMJ sagittal view shows a condyle that appears a little more
arthritic than the opposite side. There is peaking of the condyle and
anterior beaking of the condyle. Articular eminence is fairly steep.
There is decreased posterior joint space and an increased anterior joint
space through most of the joint.

Left TMJ coronal view shows a condyle with fairly good mediolateral
width. There is decreased vertical joint space. There is an extension of
the lateral pole out laterally. Lateral rim of the fossa is fairly flat.

Right condyle appears to have fairly good mediolateral width. There is
decreased superior joint space. There is a little osteophytic kind of
extrusion off the lateral pole of the condyle. Lateral rim curves
inferiorly about 2-3 mm.

Lateral cephalometric radiograph shows an AP dimension between the soft
palate and posterior pharyngeal wall of about 7 mm and in the base of
the tongue posterior pharyngeal wall of 8-9 mm. There is a slight
increased occlusal plane level in the remaining lower arch. Margaret has
basically a Class I skeletal and occlusal relationship.

There is some question as the possibility of having an allergic reaction
to certain metals. She has had previous titanium implants to the maxilla
and mandible. In the mandible, she tolerated it for several years before
the implant was removed from the right side. She apparently had 1 or 2
implants in the maxillary left side that caused problems and had to be
removed. She has had numerous teeth extracted because of pain, but there
was no definitive pathology noted with the teeth or with the bone. It is
possible that the pain could have been referred pain from the TMJ area.
It may be helpful in preparation for any potential surgery for Margaret
to go through hypersensitivity testing to determine if she is allergic
to the metals in the TMJ joint devices.
